Moving is disruptive for everyone — but children and pets experience it differently than adults. They can't read the checklist or understand why their routines are upended. With a little extra planning layered on top of the usual NYC logistics, you can keep the whole family on solid ground from the first packed box to the first night in the new place.

## Planning ahead with kids

### Tell them early and tell them honestly

Kids handle change better when they see it coming. Explain the move in age-appropriate terms as soon as you have a confirmed date. Walk them through what the new place will look like, what stays the same (school, friends, activities), and what's different. Older children often do better when they're given a concrete role — packing their own bookshelf, choosing the color of their new room, researching a nearby park.

### Protect the routine as long as possible

Bedtimes, mealtimes, and after-school pickups should stay consistent right up to move day. Disruption to daily routine is the main source of anxiety for young children, so keep it intact until the last possible moment, even when everything else is in boxes.

### Pack their room last, unpack it first

When you arrive, having a child's familiar things — their bed, stuffed animals, favorite books — set up and accessible turns an unfamiliar apartment into something that already feels like theirs. If you're using [residential moving services](/services/residential), let the crew know which boxes are headed straight to the kids' rooms so those get placed first.

## Planning ahead with pets

### Update ID tags and microchip records now

Before anything else: confirm that your pet's tags and microchip registry reflect your new address and a current phone number. In the controlled chaos of move day, doors get left open. An up-to-date ID is the most straightforward protection you have.

### Check building pet policies before move day

Many NYC co-ops and condos have breed restrictions, weight limits, or registration requirements. Confirm these with your new building's management office well in advance — not the morning of the move.

### Set up a safe room for move day

Pets do best when they're contained in a quiet, familiar space during the move itself — a bathroom with their bed, water, and litter box or a crate they know. Put a note on the door so the crew knows not to open it. The activity of furniture being carried through open doors, unfamiliar people, and the general disruption can spike anxiety in both dogs and cats.

### Stick to their schedule

Feed and walk your pets at their normal times, even when your own day is off-schedule. Skipping routines signals to animals that something is wrong. After the move, give them a few days to explore the new space at their own pace.

## What to do on move day itself

The simplest approach: arrange for kids and pets to be somewhere else for the main push. A trusted neighbor, family member, or dog walker can take charge for a few hours while the crew does the heavy lifting. It's safer for everyone — young children and anxious animals can get underfoot in a moving environment in ways that create real hazards.

If that's not possible, designate one adult whose only job is managing the kids or the pet. Everyone else focuses on the move. Trying to do both at once usually means neither gets the attention it needs.
