Moving into a co-op or condo in New York City involves a layer of building logistics that renters often encounter for the first time as new owners. The board has rules. The building has requirements. The moving company you hire has to meet them. Getting all of this coordinated before move day is what separates a smooth move-in from a stressful one.

Here's what to expect and how to prepare.

## The building package: start here

Most co-ops and condos require you to submit a move-in package to the management office or building superintendent before any move can be scheduled. The specifics vary by building, but the common elements are:

- A **Certificate of Insurance (COI)** from your moving company, naming the building or the management company as additionally insured, often with specific liability limits set by the building
- A **move-in fee** or refundable deposit, payable to the building
- Completed **move-in registration forms** with your name, unit number, estimated arrival time, and truck size
- Confirmation that the **freight elevator** is reserved for your move window

Some buildings also require the management company to approve the mover before the job. All of this paperwork should be collected and submitted at least a week before your move date, often longer.

The best way to get the requirements is to call the management office directly and ask what they need from you and your mover. Buildings vary considerably, and reading the fine print of your purchase contract may not give you the full picture.

## Certificates of Insurance

The COI is the piece that most buyers don't know to ask about until a few days before the move, which is too late for some buildings. Your moving company needs to provide this document, and it has to match your building's specific requirements, which often include named insureds, specific dollar amounts for general liability and workers' compensation, and an endorsement date that covers your move day.

An experienced mover will ask for your building's COI requirements early in the process and handle the paperwork without putting it back on you. If your mover hasn't mentioned this by the time you've confirmed a date, ask about it. Scanio handles building COIs regularly and knows how to file them quickly when timelines are tight.

## Freight elevator reservations

Co-ops and condos almost always restrict moves to the freight elevator, not the passenger elevator. Buildings often allow moves only on weekdays during business hours, though some also allow Saturday mornings. Holidays are typically blacked out.

The freight elevator is a shared resource. Other residents may be moving in or out the same week, and time slots fill up. Reserve yours as soon as you have a confirmed move date. Even if your mover can be flexible, the building's freight elevator calendar may not be.

Plan for more time than you think you'll need. A four-hour freight elevator reservation that runs short leaves your crew waiting in the lobby, and if the building has a strict cutoff, that delay can carry real consequences.

## Move-in fees and deposits

Buildings charge these in different ways. Some charge a flat, non-refundable move-in fee. Others take a refundable deposit, returned after a building staff member confirms no damage to common areas. Some charge both.

These fees are typically separate from anything you pay your mover, and they're paid directly to the building, often by certified check or electronic transfer. Confirm the amount and payment method with the management office before move day so there are no surprises at the door.

## Board approvals and what they cover

If you're moving into a co-op, you've already been through the board approval process for the purchase. That approval is for the transaction, not the move itself. The building still needs the standard move-in package from your mover.

Condo moves are generally more straightforward on the ownership side, though the building's move-in requirements are often just as detailed. Neither type of building simply lets you show up with a truck and unload.

## Coordinating the right mover

The logistics of a co-op or condo move require a moving company that knows how to work within building rules, not around them. That means handling COIs without being prompted, respecting freight elevator windows, understanding that the building staff member may need to let them in and out, and not parking in a way that creates problems with management or neighbors.

If you're doing a [residential move](/services/residential) into a building that requires all of this, it's worth asking your mover directly: how many co-op and condo moves have they done this year? Can they handle the COI? Have they worked with your management company before?

The logistics of a co-op or condo move are manageable. They just require coordination that starts earlier than most people expect, usually two to three weeks before move day, not two to three days.

## A few things to do right away

Once your purchase closes and you have a move date, do these things first:

1. Call the management office and ask for the full move-in requirements in writing.
2. Send those requirements to your mover so they can start the COI process.
3. Reserve the freight elevator for your move window.
4. Confirm any fees or deposits and how they're paid.
5. Verify that your mover is on any approved vendor list the building keeps, if one exists.

The paperwork side of a co-op or condo move takes more time than the physical move itself. Build in enough runway and it all runs smoothly.
