Walk-up apartments are a fact of life in New York. Prewar buildings without elevators make up a huge portion of the city's rental stock, and plenty of them are genuinely charming places to live. Moving into or out of one, though, is a different job than an elevator building. The stairs change how long things take, what equipment is practical, and how crews need to work. If you're planning a walk-up move, here's what to know before the truck shows up.

## Why stairs change everything

The most obvious issue is time. Every box, every piece of furniture, every appliance has to travel a staircase instead of a freight elevator. On a four-flight walk-up, that adds up. Movers carrying a sofa or a heavy wardrobe need to pace themselves, communicate, and rest between loads. A job that would take three hours in an elevator building might take five in a walk-up, and that affects labor costs and scheduling.

Beyond time, stairwells create physical constraints. A narrow turn between the first and second floor might let a couch through lengthwise but not sideways. Some pieces need partial disassembly: bed frames, sectional sofas, dining tables with removable legs. An experienced crew spots these problems before forcing anything through, which protects your furniture and the building's walls and railings.

Walk-ups in older NYC buildings also tend to have low ceilings in the stairwell, tight landings, and steep pitch. None of this is insurmountable, but it rewards movers who've done it before over crews encountering it for the first time.

## What to do before move day

A little preparation makes a real difference.

**Walk the stairs yourself.** Measure your doorway and the stairwell width, and note any tricky turns. If you're not sure a particular piece of furniture will make it, your movers can advise, but the more information you give them upfront, the better they can plan.

**Reserve the entryway.** Walk-up buildings usually don't have a formal move reservation system the way elevator buildings do, but it's worth alerting your super and blocking the entrance as best you can. Neighbors carrying groceries through the door while movers are on the stairs creates unnecessary traffic.

**Clear the stairwell.** Bikes, strollers, and boxes stored on the stairs are a liability and a delay. Ask neighbors or building management to keep landings clear on move day.

**Park close.** The truck needs to be as close to the front door as possible. In many neighborhoods that means talking to your building or calling 311 ahead of time to arrange a temporary no-parking space. The fewer steps between the truck and the door, the better.

**Think about what you actually need.** A move is a good time to be honest about a piece of furniture. If it barely fits in your current place and would require disassembly to get up four flights, it might be worth selling it and buying something better suited to the new apartment.

## Big and heavy items deserve special attention

For most furniture, an experienced crew with the right equipment handles walk-ups routinely. But some items are genuinely specialized: pianos, large marble tabletops, antique pieces that can't be disassembled, or safes. These need extra planning and sometimes custom rigging. If you have anything like this, flag it when you [request your estimate](/quote) so the crew comes prepared.

The same goes for [residential moves](/services/residential) that involve both a walk-up and some kind of storage gap. If you're vacating one apartment before the new one is ready, temperature-controlled [storage](/storage) can bridge the timing without your things sitting in a van.

## What this means for cost

Walk-up moves cost more than elevator moves because they take more time and physical effort. How much more depends on the number of floors, the volume of your belongings, and any specialty pieces. There's no simple formula, and anyone quoting you a flat rate without knowing the access details isn't quoting you accurately. Get an estimate that accounts for what's actually in your apartment and what the stairs at both ends look like.

For [long-distance moves](/services/long-distance) that start or end at a walk-up, the same applies: the access conditions matter as much as the mileage.
