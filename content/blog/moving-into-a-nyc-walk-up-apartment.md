Walk-up apartments are a fact of life in New York City. Prewar buildings without elevators make up a large share of the city's rental stock, especially in neighborhoods like the East Village, Washington Heights, Carroll Gardens, and Astoria. If you've just signed a lease in one, or you're helping someone move out of one, the process is different from a standard elevator building and worth thinking through ahead of time.

## What actually makes a walk-up move harder

The obvious part is the stairs. But the difficulty is not just about effort. A few things combine to make walk-up moves genuinely more complex:

- **Floor number.** A second-floor move is inconvenient. A fifth-floor move with a tight landing at the top is a different challenge entirely. More floors mean more trips and more time.
- **Stairwell width and turns.** Prewar stairwells are often narrow and may have sharp 90-degree turns between flights. A sofa or mattress that fits through a doorway might not clear the stairwell turn. This is where careful measuring before move day pays off.
- **No freight elevator.** In a standard building, large or heavy items go on the freight elevator. In a walk-up, everything goes up the stairs, period.
- **Street access.** Without a loading dock, movers park on the street, which means dealing with alternate-side rules, hydrant clearance, and sometimes a parking permit from the city. A good moving company handles this coordination ahead of time.

## Before you book: measure everything

The single best thing you can do before a walk-up move is measure the stairwell, the landing at each floor, and the doorways. Then measure your largest pieces of furniture. Sectionals, large wardrobes, and king-size bed frames are the usual suspects for not fitting.

If something does not fit on the stairs, you have a few options:

- Disassemble it before move day (this works for most bed frames, flat-pack furniture, and many sectionals).
- Hoist it through a window. Window hoisting requires the right equipment and experience, and not every building allows it, but it is a real solution for items that genuinely will not go up the stairs.
- Put it into storage and decide later. If you are in between places or in the middle of a renovation, our [temperature-controlled storage facility](/storage) in Secaucus, NJ can hold the piece while you work out a plan.

## What walk-ups mean for your quote

Walk-up surcharges are common and entirely legitimate. Moving a two-bedroom up four flights takes more crew time than the same move into an elevator building, and the price reflects that. The floor number, stairwell conditions, and the size and weight of your items all factor in.

## Building access and COIs

Walk-up buildings are often smaller and owner-managed, and their access requirements vary. Some require a Certificate of Insurance from the moving company before you can bring a truck to the front. Others have informal arrangements. Your moving company should ask about building requirements early and handle the COI paperwork if one is needed. We do this on [residential moves](/services/residential) every day.

One other thing to sort out ahead of time: restricted moving hours. Many NYC buildings have rules about when moves can happen (say, no moves before 9am or after 5pm on weekdays). Find out before booking. Move-day surprises like this eat into the time you're paying for.

## On move day

A few practical things that make walk-up days go smoothly:

- Clear a path through your apartment before the crew arrives. Every minute spent moving boxes out of a hallway is a minute not spent on the stairs.
- Have water on hand. Walk-ups are hard physical work, and a crew that is taking care of your things deserves the basic courtesy.
- If you have a neighbor below your new apartment, a quick heads-up the day before avoids any awkwardness from the noise.
- Label boxes with their destination room. When you are paying by the hour and everything is coming up a staircase, not having to ask where each box goes saves real time.
