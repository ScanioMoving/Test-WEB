Renovation projects in New York have a way of expanding. What starts as a kitchen gut job becomes a month of living around plastic sheeting. A bathroom remodel pushes into the hallway. Suddenly you're eating takeout in the one room that hasn't been touched, and your furniture is in the way of every contractor who shows up.

Storing furniture while a renovation is underway is a practical move. It clears the space for the work, protects your belongings from dust and damage, and often makes the project go faster because workers aren't navigating around a couch that has nowhere to go.

## When storage makes sense during a renovation

Not every project needs a full clear-out. A modest paint job or new light fixtures probably doesn't. But a few scenarios almost always call for getting furniture out of the apartment:

- **Flooring work.** Hardwood refinishing, tile replacement, or new flooring throughout the apartment. The floors need to be bare, and the smell and fumes are good reason to be out entirely.
- **Kitchen or bathroom gut renovations.** These trades work tight, generate a lot of debris, and go on longer than anyone expects.
- **Full gut renovations or gut-and-redesign projects.** If the place is coming down to the studs, there's no room to store anything on-site.
- **Relocating temporarily while work is done.** Staying with family or subletting across town is far easier if you're not hauling pieces back and forth.

## What to put in storage versus keep on hand

Think through what you'll actually need access to during the renovation window.

Put in storage: furniture, rugs, art, mirrors, and anything fragile or easily scratched. These are also the items most at risk from construction dust, which gets into everything and isn't always obvious until you wipe something down later.

Keep accessible: your day-to-day clothes, work equipment, documents, and any items you'll need at wherever you're staying. If you're doing a partial renovation and living in part of the apartment, take stock room by room.

## How the move-in and move-out piece works

For many renovation projects, the storage leg is just one piece of a larger move. You move out (into temporary housing), the work gets done, and then you move back in. Or you're renovating before you occupy a new place, so the furniture is coming off a truck and going into storage until the job is finished.

Either way, a mover who handles both the transport and the storage simplifies things considerably. One company, one point of contact, one less thing to coordinate.

## What to know about temperature-controlled storage

For furniture and household goods, temperature stability is what matters. Extreme heat causes wood to warp, finishes to crack, and upholstery to degrade over time. Extreme cold does similar damage. Our [temperature-controlled storage facility in Secaucus, NJ](/storage) keeps your belongings in a stable environment throughout the project.

A note on what "temperature-controlled" means: the facility maintains a consistent temperature range, which protects against seasonal extremes. It is not the same as climate-controlled storage that also regulates humidity. For standard furniture, temperature control is the right choice. For items extremely sensitive to humidity (certain musical instruments, some archival materials), ask about your specific piece and we'll tell you honestly what we recommend.

## How long you'll need storage

Renovation timelines in New York slip. A project quoted at six weeks often runs eight or ten. When you're planning the storage period, build in a buffer. Month-to-month storage gives you flexibility so you're not scrambling at the end of a lease period to get everything back before the contractor has signed off.
