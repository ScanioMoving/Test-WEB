## When should you book a mover in NYC?

For most moves, book **three to four weeks ahead**. Give yourself a little more lead time for a summer move or anything at the end of the month, when demand peaks and good crews fill up fast. There is such a thing as booking too early, though — plans shift, and locking in too far in advance can work against you. Three to four weeks is the sweet spot for most New Yorkers.

## Co-op and condo rules: COI, elevators, and move windows

This is where New York moves get their own rulebook, and it's where most of the day-of surprises come from. A few things to handle before move day:

- **Certificate of Insurance (COI).** Most buildings require a COI naming the building and its managing agent in very specific wording. Send your mover the building's requirements as soon as you have them so there's time to get it right.
- **Reserve the service elevator early.** Many buildings only allow moves on weekdays during set hours, and some require a deposit to hold the elevator.
- **Remember it's two buildings, not one.** The building you're leaving and the building you're moving into each have their own rules, and both sets have to be satisfied.

Scanio handles COI paperwork and building coordination as a matter of course — it's part of every NYC move we run.

## How to pack (and what you should never pack yourself)

A few rules that save a lot of damage:

- **Heavy items in small boxes, light items in large ones.** Books go in small boxes; linens and pillows in large ones.
- **Fill every box to the top.** A half-full box gets crushed when it's stacked in the truck.
- **Leave the fragile things to the pros.** Artwork, mirrors, electronics, and breakables should be packed by your movers — most companies carry only limited liability for the contents of boxes you pack yourself.
- **Carry the irreplaceables yourself.** Jewelry, cash, important documents, and medication should never go on the truck. Keep them with you.

## How to vet a moving company — and the red flags

The single most common mistake people make is choosing a mover on price alone. As Nir and Idan put it, "a quote that seems too good to be true usually is," and the difference tends to show up on moving day as extra charges, rushed crews, or damaged furniture. Here's how to protect yourself:

- **Ask for the mover's USDOT number and New York State DOT license — and verify them.** (Scanio is licensed and insured: NY DOT T11495, US DOT 537054, ICC MC93512.)
- **Ask how they handle insurance.** Do they offer additional valuation through their own carrier, or send you to a third party?
- **Insist on an in-person estimate for any sizable move, with a written inventory list.**

Red flags to walk away from: a large cash deposit, no breakdown of crew size or hours, or a quote far below everyone else's. We go deeper on this in our guide to [how to choose a moving company in NYC](/blog/how-to-choose-a-moving-company-in-nyc).

## The lesson behind every good move

One job the team still talks about was a two-week move upstate that needed a third-party crane, multiple trucks, and a temporary workshop built outside the client's home where Scanio's carpenters built custom crates by the day. The takeaway is the one we repeat to every client: a move's success starts in the planning.

## Five things to do the week before you move

1. **Confirm everything in one call.** Reconfirm the date and arrival time with your mover, the elevator reservation with both buildings, and that both buildings have received the COI.
2. **Purge what you won't need.** Donate, sell, or toss anything that isn't coming with you — every item you don't move saves time and money.
3. **Plan for the fridge and freezer.** Empty them and move the contents yourself; defrost and clean the freezer the day before.
4. **Pack an essentials bag.** Jewelry, cash, passports, sensitive documents, and daily medication — keep it with you on move day.
5. **Know where everything goes.** Decide in advance where each major piece of furniture lands in the new place, so you can direct the crew and it's right the first time.
