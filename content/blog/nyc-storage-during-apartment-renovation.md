Gut renovations and kitchen remodels happen all the time in New York apartments, but the logistics of where your furniture and boxes go while the work is happening don't always get thought through until the last minute. A contractor tearing out a bathroom needs clear access. Workers sanding floors need the room empty. And a renovation that was supposed to take six weeks has a way of stretching to twelve.

Figuring out storage before the work starts saves you a lot of scrambling.

## Why your apartment can't just hold it all

The obvious solution is to shove everything into the rooms that aren't being touched. That works for a single-room refresh, but most real renovations make it impractical fast. Dust travels everywhere. Workers need to move through the apartment. If you're doing floors, you're doing the whole floor. If you're doing the kitchen, you're living out of boxes in the living room for months.

And then there's the damage question. Construction and furniture don't mix well. Even with plastic sheeting, dust gets into fabric and finishes. A stray tool or a careless worker can scratch a table or crack a frame. Moving everything out protects what you own.

## Options worth considering

**Short-term storage off-site.** This is the cleanest solution for anything you're not actively using during the renovation. Furniture, artwork, seasonal items, anything not needed day-to-day can go into a storage unit while the work is done. [Temperature-controlled storage](/storage) matters more than people expect: adhesives, wood, leather, and electronics all react to sustained heat or cold, and a Secaucus facility like ours holds a stable range for your belongings while they're out of the apartment.

**Pod or container storage.** Some people like the idea of a portable container parked outside the building. This works in some parts of the city, but street parking rules and building regulations often make it harder in practice than it sounds. In dense neighborhoods, a container sitting in front of your building for two months creates problems. Worth checking before you commit.

**A proper pack-and-move.** If the renovation is extensive, the most efficient approach is a full pack, move to storage, and move back when the work is done. This is what [residential moving and packing services](/services/residential) are for. Everything gets wrapped, inventoried, and stored, then returned and placed when you have a clean, finished apartment again. You're not living in construction chaos and your furniture is protected the whole time.

## Things that often catch people off guard

**The timeline will slip.** Whatever the contractor tells you, build in a buffer. A one-month renovation turning into three is not unusual. Make sure your storage arrangement can extend without penalties or complicated logistics.

**You still need to live somewhere.** If the whole apartment is being renovated, storage is only part of the equation. Temporary housing in New York is expensive, so people often try to stay put longer than they should. Have a clear plan for where you're sleeping and where your daily essentials are before work starts.

**Building access rules apply here too.** Moving furniture out of an NYC apartment through the freight elevator requires the same COI and elevator reservation as any other move. A mover who knows the process handles this without you having to explain it to your building manager. **Fragile and high-value items need extra attention.** Art, antiques, and specialty pieces warrant more than just a moving blanket. If your renovation has you moving anything with real value, it's worth asking about white-glove handling. ## Planning the move-back

The move-back is often an afterthought, but it's its own job. You're placing furniture into a freshly finished space and the last thing you want is a scratch on new floors or a scuff on new paint. This is where working with movers who take it seriously pays off.

Give yourself a clear window after the contractor signs off before the movers arrive for the return. A day or two to clean and inspect the space before furniture goes back in is worth it.
