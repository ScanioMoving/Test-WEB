Downsizing is a common move in New York City. Empty nesters whose kids have left home, retirees simplifying their lives, or anyone who's decided a smaller space fits better than a larger one, all face the same basic challenge: you have more stuff than your next place can hold, and you need to figure out what goes where before the movers show up.

Done with some planning, a downsize move can be remarkably freeing. Done without it, move day becomes the day every bad decision lands at once.

## Start with the inventory, not the boxes

The biggest mistake people make is packing before they've decided what they're keeping. In a smaller apartment, furniture that worked in a larger space often won't fit at all. A king-size bed in a room that can barely hold a full, a sectional sofa that blocks every doorway, a dining table built for eight when you have a galley kitchen: these don't become smaller just because you move them.

Before you pack a single box, sketch out your new floor plan and measure the major pieces you're considering keeping. Take the actual room dimensions from the lease or from a visit to the new apartment. A tape measure and 30 minutes will save you a lot of grief.

For anything that doesn't make the cut, you have a few options:

- **Sell** items with real resale value. Facebook Marketplace and Craigslist move furniture in NYC faster than anywhere.
- **Donate** pieces in good condition. Many organizations will pick up larger items.
- **Store** things you're not ready to part with but genuinely don't need right away.

## Furniture to reconsider before moving day

Some categories almost always need reevaluation in a downsize:

**Large sofas and sectionals.** These define a room in a big apartment and overwhelm one in a small one. Measure before you commit.

**Oversized bed frames and headboards.** A king-size mattress takes up a lot of floor space in a smaller bedroom, and many prewar NYC bedrooms are already compact.

**Freestanding bookshelves and storage units.** A smaller apartment often has less wall space to absorb them. Built-in or leaner options may serve better.

**Dining room furniture.** If you're moving from a place with a dedicated dining room to one without, the table and chairs may need to go into storage or find a new home.

None of this means you have to get rid of everything. It means knowing before the movers arrive, rather than after.

## The NYC logistics that come with any downsize

A smaller apartment doesn't mean a simpler move, necessarily. Most of the logistics that come with any NYC [residential move](/services/residential) still apply.

Buildings require a Certificate of Insurance from your moving company before the move can happen. Most co-ops, condos, and even many rental buildings ask for one with specific coverage limits. A mover who handles COIs routinely will take care of this once you send them the building's requirements.

Freight elevators need to be reserved at both ends, if both buildings have them. Book early, especially if you're moving near the end of the month when move traffic peaks.

Parking for the truck is worth thinking about in advance. In dense neighborhoods, you may want to talk to the building about holding a space, or check whether a street parking permit applies.

If one apartment is a walk-up, that changes the time and cost of the move. Movers charge carry fees for stairs, typically per flight. Ask about this when you're getting estimates so the final bill matches what you expect.

## What to do with items you're not sure about

One of the more practical things storage enables is a trial period. You can move into the new apartment with just the things you know fit and work, then retrieve items from storage over the following weeks as you figure out what the space actually needs. It's a better approach than trying to force a decision while packing boxes under pressure.
