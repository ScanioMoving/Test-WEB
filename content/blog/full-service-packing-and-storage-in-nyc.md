Sometimes you need your whole home packed up and stored for a while — a renovation, a sale that closed before your next place is ready, a stint abroad, or simply downsizing with no time to deal with it. In New York City, doing that yourself means boxes, a truck, a storage unit across town, and a lot of trips. **Full-service packing and storage** replaces all of it with one coordinated service: a crew packs everything, moves it, and keeps it safe until you want it back. Here's exactly how it works, when it's worth it, and what it costs.

## What "full-service packing and storage" actually means

It's two services run as one job by the same accountable team:

1. **Professional packing** — a trained crew brings the materials and packs your entire home: fragile items, electronics, clothing, kitchen, furniture disassembly, the works. You don't touch a box.
2. **Transport and storage** — everything is loaded, inventoried, and moved into a secure, climate-controlled warehouse, where it stays until you schedule delivery to your new place (or back to the same one).

The point is that one company owns the whole chain — so nothing gets lost in a handoff between a packing service, a mover, and a storage facility.

## How the process works, step by step

1. **Survey & estimate.** We size the job from a quick video walkthrough or an itemized list — enough to know the crew, truck, materials, and roughly how much storage space you'll need.
2. **Pack day.** The crew arrives with boxes, paper, padding, and crating materials and packs the home, labeling and logging an inventory as they go.
3. **Load & transport.** Everything is padded, loaded, and driven to the warehouse the same day.
4. **Climate-controlled storage.** Your belongings are stored in our temperature-controlled facility in Secaucus, NJ — three miles from the Lincoln Tunnel. You're billed month-to-month and pay only for the space you actually use.
5. **Delivery when you're ready.** When your next place is set, we schedule redelivery and, if you'd like, unpacking.

## When it makes sense

- **Renovations** — clear the apartment, store it, bring it back when the dust settles.
- **A gap between homes** — your sale or lease ends before the next place is ready.
- **Home staging** — remove clutter and excess furniture to show a property.
- **Downsizing or estate transitions** — store what you're not ready to part with.
- **Extended travel or a work assignment abroad** — a secure place for everything while you're gone.

In most of these, you **won't need day-to-day access** to your things — which is good news for the budget (more on that below).

## What it costs — and what drives the price

There's no flat sticker price, but the cost comes down to a few clear factors:

- **Packing labor and materials.** A one-bedroom packs faster than a four-bedroom; fragile-heavy homes (art, glassware, electronics) take more care and materials.
- **The volume you store.** Storage is priced by the space your goods occupy. A studio takes far less room than a townhouse, and you pay for what you use.
- **How long you store.** Storage is month-to-month, so a two-month renovation costs far less than a year abroad.
- **Access needs.** If you won't need to retrieve items, a more remote, lower-cost warehouse is perfect — and that's usually how pack-and-store customers prefer it.
- **Building logistics.** NYC's usual variables apply on pack/move day: walk-ups vs. elevators, long carries, freight-elevator reservations, and parking. For the full picture, see [what actually drives the cost of a NYC move](/blog/how-much-do-movers-cost-in-nyc).

The honest way to get a real number is a short video walkthrough or an item list — anything less is a guess.

## A few NYC-specific things to know

- **Certificate of Insurance (COI).** Most co-op, condo, and rental buildings require the mover to file a COI naming the building before move day. A full-service mover handles this for you — just send your building's requirements.
- **Climate control matters here.** NYC humidity and temperature swings are hard on wood furniture, instruments, electronics, and art. Temperature-controlled storage protects them; a cheap non-climate unit can warp or mildew them.
- **You don't have to visit the warehouse.** Because the storage is managed, you don't need a facility near you — which is exactly why a slightly remote, secure warehouse is both safe and economical.
